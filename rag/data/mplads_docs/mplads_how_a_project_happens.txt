MPLADS - How a Project Happens
A narrative walkthrough from idea to finished asset
Note: Compiled from public sources (MoSPI MPLADS Guidelines, PIB releases, mplads.gov.in and secondary
summaries) as of October 2026. This is an unofficial knowledge-base document. For binding rules always refer to the
latest official Guidelines/circulars issued by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI).
Starting with a local need
Every MPLADS project begins with a local need. A village may lack a proper approach road, a government
school may have too few classrooms, a health centre may need an ambulance, or a neighbourhood may
need a community toilet. Residents, panchayats, local bodies and community groups usually raise these
needs with their Member of Parliament. The District Authority also keeps a shelf of projects, which is a
suggestive list of possible works, including projects for areas inhabited by Scheduled Castes and
Scheduled Tribes. The shelf is only a guide, and the Member is free to recommend works beyond it.
It is important to understand that the Member of Parliament is the only person who can formally
recommend a work. A request from a resident, a local leader or even a representative of the Member does
not by itself start the process, and the District Authority cannot act on recommendations from third parties.
The recommendation
The Member recommends the work to the District Authority of the nodal district, specifying the nature of
the work, its location and approximate cost, and the order of priority among several works. Under the older
paper-based system this was done through a signed letter on the Member's letterhead. Since 2023,
recommendations are made through the eSAKSHI portal or its mobile app. The Member must make sure
that the amounts recommended do not exceed the annual entitlement and that, across the year, at least
15 percent goes to works in Scheduled Caste areas and at least 7.5 percent to works in Scheduled Tribe
areas.
The Member must also set aside the full estimated cost of the work. If the amount indicated is less than the
estimate and no other source can cover the shortfall, the work cannot be sanctioned, because it would be
left half-finished. In such a case the District Authority informs the Member of the shortfall within the time
allowed.
Scrutiny and sanction
Once the recommendation reaches the District Authority, which is usually the District Collector or District
Magistrate, the office examines whether the work is eligible under the guidelines, whether the necessary
clearances are available, and whether an agency such as a school, hospital or panchayat is willing to
operate and maintain the asset after it is built. If the work is not feasible or not eligible, the District Authority
must tell the Member the reasons within 45 days. If it is acceptable, the work must be sanctioned within 75
days of receiving the recommendation. The sanction order names the implementing agency, states the
approved cost, and sets a deadline for completion, which should generally not be more than one year.
State Governments are expected to delegate full powers for technical, financial and administrative
sanctions to the District Authority so that decisions can be taken quickly at the district level. The time spent
under the Model Code of Conduct during elections is not counted in these deadlines.
Execution and payment
The implementing agency, such as a public works department, a rural development agency, a municipal
body or, in some cases, a Central organisation like the Railways, carries out the work following the State
Government's rules on estimates, tendering and standards. At various stages of execution the agency
uploads photographs and documents to the eSAKSHI portal and raises payment requests in line with the
sanction order. The District Authority reviews progress with agencies regularly, inspects a portion of the
works, and invites the Member to take part in inspections and review meetings where possible.
While the work is under way, the site or the nature of the work cannot be changed once expenditure
liability has been incurred. A Member may cancel a sanctioned work only if execution has not begun and
the cancellation does not create any financial liability for the government.
Completion and handover
When the work is finished, the implementing agency prepares a completion report and a utilisation
certificate, returns any unused money, and the asset is handed over to the user agency, which takes it on
its books for regular operation and upkeep. A permanent plaque is placed at the site carrying the words
Member of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme Work, along with the cost, the dates of
commencement, completion and inauguration, and the name of the Member who recommended the work.
The asset is entered in the district's asset register, and the work is shown as completed on the public
dashboard.
