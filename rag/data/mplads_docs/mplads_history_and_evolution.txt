MPLADS - History and Evolution
How the scheme began and changed over three decades
Note: Compiled from public sources (MoSPI MPLADS Guidelines, PIB releases, mplads.gov.in and secondary
summaries) as of October 2026. This is an unofficial knowledge-base document. For binding rules always refer to the
latest official Guidelines/circulars issued by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI).
Origins of the scheme
The Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme was announced in Parliament by the Prime
Minister on 23 December 1993. The idea behind it was simple: people routinely approach their elected
representatives for basic facilities such as drinking water, classrooms, roads and health services, and the
scheme gave Members of Parliament a dedicated source of funds to respond to these locally felt needs. In
its first year, 1993-94, each Member was allotted Rs. 5 lakh, and the scheme was initially placed under the
Ministry of Rural Development.
The first set of guidelines was issued in February 1994 and covered the concept, implementation and
monitoring of the scheme. In October 1994 the scheme was transferred to the Ministry of Statistics and
Programme Implementation, which has remained its nodal ministry ever since. The ministry is responsible
for framing policy, authorising funds and prescribing how the scheme is monitored.
Growth in the annual entitlement
From 1994-95 the entitlement was raised to Rs. 1 crore per Member per year. It was stepped up to Rs. 2
crore from 1998-99, and from the financial year 2011-12 it has stood at Rs. 5 crore per Member per year.
The amount is the same for every Member, whether the constituency is rural or urban and regardless of
how large its population is. The Government has stated that it receives and examines suggestions from
stakeholders, including proposals to revise the entitlement, but at the time of the latest official statements
the amount remained Rs. 5 crore.
Across the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, this adds up to an annual outlay of roughly Rs. 4,000 crore,
which makes MPLADS one of the larger local-development funding channels run directly through elected
representatives.
Revisions of the guidelines
The guidelines have been revised many times as experience accumulated. Editions followed in December
1994, February 1997, September 1999, April 2002, November 2005, August 2012 and May 2014, and a
comprehensive revision was issued in June 2016. These revisions drew on recommendations of the Lok
Sabha and Rajya Sabha committees on MPLADS, observations of the Comptroller and Auditor General,
feedback from State Governments during review meetings and suggestions from Members of Parliament
themselves. Over time the guidelines added special provisions, for example for conserving water through
rainwater harvesting, for accessibility for persons with disabilities, for sanitation drives and for
convergence with other national programmes.
The most recent major revision took effect on 1 April 2023. It introduced stricter timelines for sanctioning
and completing works, stronger monitoring duties for Central and State departments and implementing
agencies, provisions for periodic inspection, documentation and auditing, and a penal clause for material
breach of the guidelines. It also moved the entire process online through the eSAKSHI portal.
The COVID-19 suspension and restoration
In April 2020 the Government suspended MPLADS for the financial years 2020-21 and 2021-22 so that
roughly Rs. 8,000 crore could be diverted to the response to the COVID-19 pandemic. In November 2021
the scheme was restored, and Members were given Rs. 2 crore each for the remaining part of 2021-22.
From 2022-23 the full entitlement of Rs. 5 crore per Member per year resumed. During the suspension
period the Government also allowed one-time dispensations, such as permitting funds for medical testing
and screening equipment for government hospitals.
This episode is useful to remember when answering questions from the public, because works
recommended during or around those years may have been affected, and because older records may not
show the same patterns of fund release as later years.
The move to eSAKSHI in 2023
Until 2022-23, funds were released in two instalments of Rs. 2.5 crore each to the bank account of the
District Authority of the Member's nodal district, and the process of recommending, sanctioning and paying
for works relied heavily on paperwork. From 1 April 2023, with the launch of the eSAKSHI portal, the
annual entitlement is authorised in a single instalment of Rs. 5 crore at the start of each financial year or at
the start of the Member's term, under the revised fund-flow procedure aligned with Ministry of Finance
guidelines.
Because the portal was launched in 2023, detailed online data for the 17th Lok Sabha is available only
from 2023-24 onward, and for Rajya Sabha Members only from that period as well. Anyone researching
earlier years will need to rely on older reports and district records rather than the dashboard.
